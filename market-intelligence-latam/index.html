Start with the business question

Research is useful when it changes a decision.

A market can look attractive in a slide deck and still be wrong for the company, price point or moment. The first step is to state the decision precisely enough that evidence can rule options in or out.

01 / ENTRY

Where should we enter?

Compare countries, sectors, demand signals, constraints and timing before turning “Latin America” into one undifferentiated market.

02 / PRICE

What price makes sense?

Map visible market prices, competitor offers, customer context and economic conditions without pretending a benchmark is a strategy by itself.

03 / COMPETITION

Who actually matters?

Separate the competitors that shape the decision from the long list that merely shares a category label.

04 / CONTEXT

What changed?

Trace regulation, inflation, credit, sector signals and other external changes back to the decision your team has to make now.

Decision-grade deliverables

A memo you can use in the meeting.

The output is sized to the question. A focused issue may need a compact decision brief; a market-entry question may need a deeper evidence pack and a working dashboard.

Decision briefExecutive synthesis: what the evidence says, what remains uncertain and what the decision hinges on.
Market mapCompetitors, offers, prices, segments, channels or country comparisons relevant to the question.
Evidence appendixSources, definitions, calculations and assumptions kept visible enough to challenge.
DashboardWhen the decision needs recurring monitoring, a compact visual layer can make the changing signals easier to see.
How the work moves

Four steps. One question stays in view.

Frame

Define the decision, alternatives, geography, time horizon and what would count as useful evidence.

Map

Build the source and competitor map before collecting facts indiscriminately.

Test

Compare the evidence against the working assumptions and make uncertainty explicit.

Deliver

Return the findings in a format that can be discussed, challenged and acted on by the team.

This is research and decision support. Regulatory, tax and legal questions are mapped as business context; formal legal or tax advice belongs with the appropriate licensed professional.
