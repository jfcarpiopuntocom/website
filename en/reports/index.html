An analysis you can put to work

You receive a written report on the agreed question. It brings together findings, sources and assumptions, compares the relevant options and explains what to do next. Where the evidence is insufficient, it says so.

  • The question and what information was used.
  • What the figures or sources show, and what they leave unresolved.
  • Options, tradeoffs and a recommendation tied to the available evidence.
  • A practical next step and what to check before acting.
Read a worked example: the cost of a discount

Illustrative example. Not a customer case or a promised result.

The question

A shop sells a product for USD 20. Its variable cost is USD 12. Would a 10% discount help the business?

What the figures show

At the current price, each unit contributes USD 8. At USD 18, it contributes USD 6. Selling 100 units leaves USD 800 before fixed expenses; at the discounted price, 100 units leave USD 600.

The shop would need to sell at least 134 units at USD 18 to match the original USD 800 contribution. A 10% discount would require roughly 34% more units in this example, assuming the variable cost stays at USD 12.

What still needs checking

The arithmetic does not tell us whether demand would rise that much. We would need to check stock, capacity, returns and any costs omitted from the USD 12. If clearing slow stock is the objective, the decision needs that context too.

A next step

Compare a limited discount test with keeping the price and changing the offer. Define what you will measure before spending. A report on your business would use the agreed inputs and explain the limits of the recommendation.
