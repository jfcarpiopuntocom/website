Where Latin America ranks in critical minerals.

The United States lists 60 critical minerals. We went through the country tables in the US Geological Survey's 2026 report and asked one question for each mineral: is a Latin American country among the world's three largest producers?

The JFCarpio.com LatAm Top-Three Count 13 of 60

Critical minerals on the US 2025 list for which a Latin American country ranks among the world's top three producers.

Chile, Peru, Mexico or Brazil ranks first in niobium, silver, arsenic, copper and rhenium.

Six findings

Five minerals led by the region. Eight more in the top three.

5 of 13

The region leads five minerals

Brazil mines 92.9% of the world's niobium. Mexico leads in silver, Peru in arsenic, and Chile in both copper and rhenium.

52.3%

Half of the world's silver

Mexico, Peru, Bolivia, Chile and Argentina together mine 52.3% of world silver. Mexico alone accounts for 24.2%, and Peru ranks second.

37.8%

More than a third of mined copper

Chile leads with 23.0% of world mine output and Peru ranks third with 11.7%. Copper joined the US list in 2025.

5 of 10

Half of the 2025 additions

The 2025 list added ten minerals to the 2022 list. A Latin American country ranks in the top three for five of them: copper, lead, rhenium, silicon and silver.

31.4%

Lithium: a third, but not the lead

Chile, Argentina and Brazil produce 31.4% of world lithium. Chile ranks third, behind Australia and China.

7 of 8

China leads where the region follows

In the eight minerals where Latin America is in the top three but not first, China leads seven. Australia leads the eighth, lithium.

Method

How we counted.

  • Source: US Geological Survey, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, world production tables, estimated 2025 figures. The list of 60 minerals is the US Final 2025 List of Critical Minerals (90 FR 50494, November 7, 2025), as reprinted in that report.
  • Latin America here means the countries of South America, Central America, Mexico and the Caribbean. Shares add every Latin American country USGS lists for that mineral and divide by the USGS world total.
  • The count is a minimum. We left out beryllium and boron, where USGS reports different products for each country or the table does not give a clean 2025 ranking. Copper is mine output; silicon and magnesium are metal output.
  • Ties count as shared places: Mexico ties Mongolia in fluorspar, and Brazil ties Israel in magnesium metal.
  • Several minerals on the list, such as uranium and metallurgical coal, have no USGS production table. Rare earths and platinum-group metals are each reported as a group.
